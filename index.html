SB-CFM listening test

Help us rate synthesised sound effects

You will hear short recordings of everyday sounds and rate each one on two scales. It takes about 20 minutes. Your answers are anonymous and are used only in aggregate, for a research paper on sound synthesis.

Before you start
  • Wear headphones. Laptop and phone speakers hide exactly the differences we are asking about. There is a short check on the next page.
  • Find a quiet place and set your volume to a comfortable level. Keep it there for the whole session — changing it mid-way makes your ratings inconsistent.
  • Use a desktop or laptop if you can.
  • Finish in one sitting. Progress is saved in this browser, so you can reload if something goes wrong, but a different device starts over.

Participation is voluntary and you may close the page at any time; nothing is recorded unless you reach the final screen and submit. We collect only your ratings, timing, browser type, and screen size. No name, email, or IP address is stored.

Step 2 of 4 — what to listen for

Two things to judge, separately

Sound quality

How clean the recording is: free of artefacts, fuzziness, distortion, hiss, or a muffled, smeared quality. Judge only how it sounds, not what it is. A crisp recording of the wrong thing still scores high here.

Category fit

How readily you would call the sound by the name we give you. Use your general sense of what that category sounds like — there is no single correct example, and the number of events in the clip does not matter. A clip with one footstep and a clip with twenty can both fit perfectly.

Both scales run from 0 to 10. Treat 10 as the best a sound could possibly be, not the best in this test. Most clips will land in between — please use the whole range rather than clustering in the middle.

You can replay a clip as often as you like. There is no time limit, but go with your first impression; deliberating rarely improves these judgements.
